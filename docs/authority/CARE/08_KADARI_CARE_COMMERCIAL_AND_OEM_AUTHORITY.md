# KADARI Care — Commercial & OEM Authority

**Status:** Founder/commercial authority  
**Date:** 2026-10-06  
**Scope:** Defines how KADARI Care monetises before and after OEM participation without making retailer or OEM integration a prerequisite to customer value.

## 1. Core doctrine

KADARI Care must create useful customer outcomes before an OEM, retailer, distributor or repair network signs a contract.

Commercial sequence:
1. customer self-captures asset;
2. KADARI finds / improves coverage;
3. KADARI handles Care events through verified public or official channels;
4. real interaction volume accumulates by brand / group / category;
5. OEM is invited into KADARI because its customers are already there;
6. basic OEM participation is free where it reduces friction and improves resolution;
7. OEMs pay for operational automation, integrations, service-network control and intelligence;
8. KADARI earns transaction/service revenue where appropriate and transparent.

Do not build a commercial model that requires enterprise contracts before the network has utility.

## 2. What KADARI monetises

KADARI monetises lifecycle orchestration around the asset, including:
- coverage activation;
- warranty registration;
- maintenance;
- fault intake;
- case preparation;
- routing;
- communications;
- repair / replacement;
- protection products;
- resale / transfer;
- replacement commerce;
- business Care workflows;
- aggregated lifecycle intelligence.

The commercial unit is not just a warranty claim.

## 3. Revenue before OEM contracts

### 3.1 Premium Care / Concierge
Ordinary self-service Care should remain useful without payment.

Optional paid assistance may include:
- human case review;
- warranty dispute support;
- document reconstruction;
- escalation preparation;
- complex multi-party coordination;
- premium case handling.

### 3.2 Repair / service orchestration
Where no free warranty route applies, KADARI may route work to verified service providers.

Preferred economics:
- fixed fee on completed work; or
- percentage of completed service value.

Avoid monetising raw leads where possible. Prefer completed-value economics.

### 3.3 Extended protection / service plans
KADARI may distribute appropriately structured protection products through licensed / suitable partners.

Potential economics:
- referral / distribution commission;
- administration technology fee;
- servicing fee.

KADARI should not initially take underwriting risk.

### 3.4 Replacement commerce
Where repair is unavailable or uneconomic, KADARI may present replacement options.

Potential economics:
- referral fee;
- affiliate fee;
- merchant acquisition fee;
- transaction commission.

Commercial placement must be disclosed and must not override customer-first recommendations.

### 3.5 Verified resale / transfer
Future revenue may include paid verified transfer / resale products for higher-value assets.

Basic household/family transfer should not automatically be monetised.

### 3.6 Business / professional Care
Businesses managing asset estates may pay for:
- team workflows;
- warranty registers;
- maintenance queues;
- multi-site case handling;
- reporting;
- governance;
- API/export capability.

This can generate revenue independently of OEM contracts.

## 4. OEM commercial ladder

### Tier 0 — Public Route
No OEM agreement required.

KADARI uses verified official/public channels under customer authority.

OEM pays nothing and receives no dedicated surface.

Purpose: prove demand and accumulate evidence.

### Tier 1 — KADARI OEM Free
Purpose: remove friction once KADARI-originated volume exists.

Potential features:
- inbox for the OEM's KADARI cases;
- structured product identity;
- customer-authorised proof pack;
- issue summary;
- request more information;
- update case state;
- add service reference;
- nominate service centre / appointment;
- close case with outcome;
- basic KADARI brand-volume summary;
- limited users.

Free does not include:
- advanced automation;
- APIs/webhooks;
- custom workflows;
- advanced analytics;
- benchmarking;
- bulk recall tooling;
- enterprise SLA;
- white-label / embedded capability.

Reason to keep this free: every case resolved directly inside KADARI improves customer outcomes, reduces Care Ops effort and increases structured data quality.

### Tier 2 — KADARI Care Pro
Paid operational tier for brands, distributors and warranty administrators.

Potential capabilities:
- team roles and queues;
- assignment;
- configurable workflows;
- automation rules;
- service-centre routing;
- response templates;
- internal notes;
- commitment / SLA tracking;
- exports;
- brand/product dashboards;
- warranty validation workflows;
- registration workflow tools;
- controlled recall / campaign capability;
- advanced reporting;
- multi-brand portfolio support.

Preferred commercial form:
- recurring platform fee;
- optional usage bands only where usage materially drives KADARI cost.

Avoid punitive per-registration economics that discourage adoption.

### Tier 3 — KADARI Care Enterprise
For high-volume OEMs, brand groups, distributors and complex service organisations.

Potential capabilities:
- APIs / webhooks;
- CRM / warranty-system integrations;
- SSO;
- enterprise roles/governance;
- custom retention/SLA;
- service-network orchestration;
- direct entitlement checks;
- automated registration validation;
- recall / remediation campaigns;
- bulk model feeds;
- dealer/distributor structures;
- dedicated analytics;
- warehouse exports;
- embedded / white-label functions where strategically justified.

Commercial form:
- annual enterprise licence;
- implementation/integration fee where real engineering is required;
- optional usage bands;
- optional intelligence module.

## 5. KADARI Care Intelligence

Operational case handling and lifecycle intelligence are separate value pools.

Potential intelligence:
- active assets by model/family;
- registration rate;
- warranty activation rate;
- fault rate;
- median age at first fault;
- common issue classes;
- parts delays;
- time to first response;
- time to resolution;
- repeat contact;
- repair vs replacement outcomes;
- service-centre performance;
- rejection reasons;
- repair cost where evidenced;
- repeat fault after repair;
- replacement timing.

Rules:
- do not sell identifiable household inventories as a data product;
- aggregated/governed insight by default;
- identifiable customer data only under a lawful relationship and appropriate authority;
- distinguish KADARI-observed, customer-declared and OEM-supplied information.

## 6. Retailer model

Retailer integration is optional, not foundational.

Initial retailer participation can be as simple as:
- QR / link at point of sale;
- receipt sticker;
- cashier/salesperson prompt;
- KADARI capture after purchase;
- retailer recognised from receipt.

Retailers may later receive value from:
- reduced after-sales handling;
- replacement opportunities;
- service-plan distribution;
- product failure intelligence;
- verified return packs;
- customer re-engagement where customer permission exists.

Possible retailer economics:
- free/basic participation;
- platform subscription for operational tooling;
- share in protection / replacement / service transactions;
- enterprise integration where justified.

## 7. Repairer / service-provider model

KADARI should not become an unmanaged open marketplace at launch.

Prioritise:
1. OEM-authorised routes while in warranty;
2. policy/plan-approved routes where cover applies;
3. verified independent providers out of warranty.

Potential economics:
- fixed completed-job fee;
- percentage of completed work;
- subscription for high-volume provider workflow later.

Do not rank providers purely by who pays KADARI most.

## 8. Consumer pricing principle

The basic KADARI asset record and ordinary Care utility should remain broadly accessible.

Potential consumer paid layers:
- premium concierge;
- advanced household Care plans;
- bundled maintenance services;
- verified resale / transfer products;
- optional premium support.

Do not make basic warranty awareness or correct routing a paid feature.

## 9. Pricing hypothesis, not yet locked

Initial OEM pricing should be validated against real case volumes and savings rather than invented in advance.

Working commercial ranges for testing only:
- Care Pro: low-to-mid five figures ZAR per month depending on brand group, seats and volume;
- Enterprise: negotiated annual contract plus integration/services where required;
- smaller distributor portfolios: lower entry pricing where one operator represents multiple brands.

Do not publish these externally until willingness-to-pay research and live pilot evidence exist.

## 10. Cost model

KADARI must measure unit economics by Care event.

Track:
- AI extraction cost;
- human Care Ops minutes;
- messaging cost;
- storage/evidence cost;
- registry maintenance cost;
- third-party data/service cost;
- payment cost where relevant;
- support cost;
- revenue generated per event.

Target: automation should progressively reduce human minutes per resolved case.

## 11. Commercial north-star metrics

Track at minimum:
- active Care-enabled assets;
- new assets/month;
- warranty value under management;
- extra cover activated;
- cases/month;
- case resolution rate;
- time to resolution;
- customer savings/covered value where evidenced;
- unknown-route rate;
- Care Ops minutes/case;
- cost per resolved case;
- revenue per active asset;
- revenue per resolved case;
- OEMs participating free;
- OEMs on Pro;
- OEMs on Enterprise;
- partner transaction revenue.

## 12. Strategic boundary

KADARI should earn because it reduces complexity, saves money, improves resolution and produces useful lifecycle intelligence.

Do not build the business around selling personal data.

Do not force integrations before value exists.

Do not make OEM participation a condition of consumer usefulness.

**Proof before partnership. Utility before enterprise dependence.**
