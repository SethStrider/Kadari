# KADARI Care — OEM Activation Playbook

**Status:** Commercial execution authority  
**Date:** 2026-10-06

## 1. Purpose

This file defines when and how KADARI should approach OEMs, distributors and brand groups once customer activity creates real leverage.

The objective is to avoid premature enterprise selling while also avoiding the opposite error of waiting too long after brand-level volume becomes meaningful.

## 2. OEM Readiness Score

Use a composite score rather than a single threshold.

Suggested inputs:
- active KADARI assets for the brand/group;
- new assets captured per month;
- Care cases in last 30 / 90 days;
- KADARI-originated contacts into OEM public channels;
- evidence completeness rate;
- percentage of cases with external references;
- resolution rate;
- average resolution time;
- unresolved-friction rate;
- Care Ops manual minutes attributable to that OEM;
- number of repeated customer information requests that KADARI could pre-supply;
- strategic value of the parent/distributor group;
- number of brands controlled by one commercial relationship.

## 3. Practical activation thresholds

These are internal operating triggers, not promises.

### Watch
Examples:
- 50+ active assets;
- 5+ Care cases in 90 days;
- repeated use of the same OEM support path.

Action:
- improve registry quality;
- collect friction data;
- identify brand owner / local operator / service network;
- do not necessarily sell yet.

### Relationship-ready
Any combination such as:
- 250+ active assets;
- 25+ Care cases in 90 days;
- 10+ monthly KADARI-originated support interactions;
- material repeated manual friction;
- group relationship capable of unlocking several brands.

Action:
- introduce KADARI;
- show factual activity;
- offer basic OEM participation / response surface;
- ask how their current process works;
- do not lead with a large licence proposal.

### Pilot-ready
Examples:
- 1,000+ active assets; or
- 75+ Care cases in 90 days; or
- enough recurring case volume to create measurable operational savings.

Action:
- offer KADARI OEM Free immediately;
- propose a 60–90 day operational pilot where useful;
- measure response time, evidence completeness, repeat contacts, resolution and manual effort.

### Commercial-ready
Examples:
- sustained monthly case volume;
- multiple OEM users active in the free surface;
- clear workflow/automation requests;
- demonstrated reduction in handling effort;
- identifiable analytics value;
- need for API, recall tools, multi-brand management or advanced reporting.

Action:
- move to Pro / Enterprise commercial proposal.

## 4. First OEM approach

Do not pitch theory first.

Lead with evidence:

> KADARI currently has X of your products under management, with Y Care interactions over the last 90 days. We are already helping your customers prepare serials, proof of purchase and issue evidence before they reach your support channels. We'd like to give your team a direct way to handle those customers inside KADARI at no cost.

Show:
- active assets;
- product/category breakdown;
- number of cases;
- top route types;
- evidence completeness;
- common causes of repeated contact;
- average response/resolution where observed;
- anonymised examples of unnecessary friction.

Do not expose another brand's confidential or identifiable information.

## 5. The free OEM offer

The free offer should solve a real problem on day one.

Minimum OEM Free workflow:
1. OEM user signs in;
2. sees only authorised cases for its brand/group;
3. opens structured case;
4. sees product identity and proof pack;
5. responds / requests evidence;
6. assigns reference / service route;
7. records appointment/service centre;
8. updates state;
9. closes with outcome.

The free dashboard should show basic factual volume but not advanced intelligence.

## 6. Pilot metrics

Before and during pilot, establish baseline where possible.

Measure:
- first-response time;
- total resolution time;
- contacts per case;
- percentage arriving with correct serial;
- percentage arriving with valid proof of purchase;
- percentage requiring repeat evidence requests;
- routing error rate;
- no-response / abandonment rate;
- service-centre appointment time;
- Care Ops minutes/case;
- OEM staff minutes/case if measurable;
- customer satisfaction / outcome confirmation;
- percentage resolved without telephone interaction where applicable.

The commercial case should emerge from measured operational improvement.

## 7. Conversion from Free to Pro

Do not cripple the free tier artificially.

Convert when OEM asks for or clearly benefits from:
- multiple queues/teams;
- routing automation;
- SLA management;
- bulk service-centre administration;
- registration validation;
- recall/campaign tooling;
- advanced analytics;
- exports;
- larger user base;
- multi-brand management;
- custom workflows.

Pitch:

> Your team is already resolving KADARI cases. Pro removes the manual steps and gives you operational control across the volume.

## 8. Conversion from Pro to Enterprise

Enterprise trigger:
- high volume;
- CRM/warranty-system integration requested;
- SSO/security requirements;
- complex distributor/dealer structure;
- direct entitlement checks;
- bulk model/product feeds;
- custom recall workflows;
- data warehouse/reporting requirements;
- contractual SLA/governance needs.

Enterprise should remove duplicate work between KADARI and the OEM's existing systems rather than replacing those systems unnecessarily.

## 9. Parent-group strategy

Always research whether one local relationship controls several brands.

Prioritise group-level relationships where appropriate.

Examples from SA research include:
- Defy / Beko / Grundig under shared local infrastructure;
- De'Longhi / Kenwood / Braun sharing service infrastructure;
- Home of Living Brands handling several major consumer brands;
- BSH structures across Bosch / Siemens / Gaggenau.

Commercially, one group relationship may unlock:
- several brand inboxes;
- one service network;
- one registry-validation source;
- one integration;
- one contract.

## 10. OEM objection handling

### “We already have a warranty platform.”
Response:
KADARI does not require the OEM to replace it. KADARI can remain the customer-side asset record and pass structured cases into the existing platform later.

### “Customers should use our app/site.”
Response:
Many KADARI customers already own multiple brands. KADARI organises the owner's whole asset estate while still routing the OEM's products into the OEM's official process.

### “We don't want another integration.”
Response:
No integration is required for OEM Free. The OEM can work cases directly in KADARI. Integration is considered only after there is enough volume to justify it.

### “We can't share customer data.”
Response:
KADARI's starting relationship is with the customer. Only customer-authorised, case-relevant data is presented to the OEM. Broader data exchange requires a separate lawful basis/agreement.

### “Why should we pay?”
Response:
Do not ask them to pay until paid capability demonstrably saves handling effort, provides operational control or supplies valuable intelligence.

## 11. Retailer activation playbook

Retailer relationship should similarly begin without POS integration.

Low-friction partnership:
- QR / receipt instruction;
- KADARI self-capture;
- retailer recognised from receipt;
- retailer-return proof pack;
- customer routed back correctly when retailer is the proper route.

Once meaningful volume exists, show retailer:
- KADARI assets purchased there;
- return/warranty cases;
- replacement timing;
- common product issues;
- repeat sales opportunity, subject to customer permissions.

Only then explore deeper integration.

## 12. Repair network activation

Do not recruit thousands of repairers before demand exists.

Build supply around observed case demand by geography/category.

Provider onboarding should verify:
- business identity;
- category capability;
- OEM authorisation where claimed;
- geography;
- pricing/quote behaviour;
- insurance/licensing where relevant;
- service warranty;
- customer outcome history.

Route in-warranty work according to OEM rules before independent alternatives.

## 13. Commercial evidence pack

For each priority brand/group maintain a live commercial dossier:
- brand/group identity;
- local decision maker targets;
- active assets;
- monthly capture trend;
- Care cases;
- public channels used;
- manual friction;
- case outcomes;
- average response/resolution;
- Care Ops cost attributable to brand;
- estimated savings opportunity;
- registry gaps;
- relationship status;
- next action.

This dossier becomes the factual basis of OEM outreach.

## 15. Commercial principle

The strongest OEM sale is not:

> “KADARI can theoretically help your customers.”

It is:

> “Your customers already use KADARI. Here is the volume. Here is the friction. Here is what happens when your team joins the same workflow.”

**Proof before partnership. Participation before integration. Operational value before licence expansion.**
