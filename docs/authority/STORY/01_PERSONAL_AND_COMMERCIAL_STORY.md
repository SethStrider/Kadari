# KADARI — Personal & Commercial Storytelling Authority

## Why storytelling matters
KADARI must not be explained only through features. The product should be understood through recognisable human and business situations.

The original AssetSure concept established the right storytelling pattern: life changes continuously while records are often static, and evidence is normally reconstructed only after something goes wrong.

KADARI expands that story beyond insurance.

---

# PERSONAL STORY — SARAH

Sarah has a home, a family and years of life inside it.

She did not buy everything in one day.

She built her life one possession at a time:
- television
- fridge
- laptop
- phone
- furniture
- jewellery
- art
- children’s things
- appliances
- tools
- vehicles

Hundreds of small purchases quietly become a household.

But the records do not evolve with the household.

Receipts disappear.
Values change.
Assets are replaced.
New things arrive.
Serial numbers are forgotten.
Photos sit on phones.
Insurance may reduce everything to one number.

Then one day somebody asks:
- What exactly was there?
- What model was it?
- Do you have the receipt?
- What was the serial number?
- What was it worth?
- What condition was it in?

The core question:

**Why are we building the evidence after the event?**

KADARI changes the starting point.

**Walk. Scan. Confirm.**

The camera sees the physical world. KADARI identifies assets, starts their Digital Twins, preserves evidence and builds Sarah’s Asset Position over time.

The value stack for Sarah includes:
- know what she owns
- know what it is worth
- retain receipts and documents
- keep warranties
- preserve condition evidence
- improve insurance readiness
- support claims
- record assets before moving or storage
- prepare estate information
- support sale or transfer
- retain theft / recovery evidence
- understand the total household Asset Position

Personal positioning:

**A living digital record of the physical life you have built.**

# COMMERCIAL STORY — THE BUSINESS

A business does not acquire its asset estate in one day either.

Over years it accumulates:
- vehicles
- plant
- tractors
- machinery
- tools
- generators
- compressors
- IT equipment
- furniture
- specialist equipment
- leased assets
- financed assets

Accounting knows some of the numbers.
Insurance has a schedule.
Operations knows where some equipment is.
Employees have custody of assets.
Invoices sit across systems.
Serial numbers sit on machines.
Photos sit on phones.
Maintenance records sit elsewhere.

The company may have multiple branches and locations, but one physical asset estate.

The commercial question becomes:

**Does the business actually know its physical asset position?**

KADARI creates the physical truth layer behind the financial record.

A commercial dashboard may ultimately show:
- total assets
- total Asset Position
- Evidenced Asset Position
- Position Confidence
- replacement-value gaps
- assets requiring updated condition
- stale valuations
- missing documents
- custody discrepancies
- ghost assets
- unrecorded assets

Commercial positioning:

**Your accounting system knows the number. KADARI knows the asset behind it.**

The business value stack includes:
- fixed asset evidence
- branch / location visibility
- custody
- book-to-floor reconciliation
- floor-to-book reconciliation
- insurance schedules
- statements of values
- year-end verification
- audit evidence
- condition records
- depreciation context
- valuation freshness
- replacement planning
- insurance alignment
- maintenance history
- asset transfers
- acquisition / disposal workflows
- sale / transaction schedules
- finance evidence
- multi-site visibility

## Important storytelling rule
Personal and Commercial should share one underlying product engine, but the front-end story should not force a business customer through a household-insurance narrative or force an individual through enterprise language.
